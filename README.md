📊 NovaRetail+ — Behavior and Revenue Analysis
🎯 Objective

To analyze which customer behavior factors are most strongly associated with annual revenue generated on NovaRetail+, an e-commerce platform operating in Latin America.

The project employs a correlational and exploratory approach; therefore, the results identify associations between variables but do not establish causal relationships.

🔎 Key Findings
`monthly_purchases` shows a very strong positive monotonic association with `annual_revenue` (Spearman ρ = 0.97).
`monthly_visits` shows a low-to-moderate positive association with `annual_revenue` (ρ = 0.32).
`targeted_ad_spend` shows a moderate positive linear association with `monthly_visits` (Pearson r = 0.58).
Most other variables show weak associations with `annual_revenue`, requiring segmented analysis to identify patterns that simple correlation cannot reveal.
Categorical variables analyzed using Cramér's V show no significant associations with one another.
🧠 Methodology

The analysis includes:

Data exploration and cleaning.
Validation of data types and missing values.
Descriptive statistical analysis.
Pearson correlation for linear relationships.
Spearman correlation for monotonic relationships.
Point-biserial correlation for binary and numerical variables.
Cramér's V for categorical variables.
Visualization using heatmaps and scatter plots.
Identification of findings and definition of future analyses.
📌 Next Steps

Further analysis is proposed through segmentation by:

Region.
Device type.
Age.
Premium membership.
Visit and purchase behavior.
Churn/Abandonment.

This will allow for the identification of customer profiles with distinct behavioral patterns and a more detailed evaluation of their relationship with annual revenue.

Note: The correlations found represent statistical associations. They should not be interpreted as evidence of causality.
